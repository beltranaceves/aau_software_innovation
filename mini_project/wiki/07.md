## 1. Strategy in Essence

> “Plans are useless, but planning is indispensable.” – Dwight D. Eisenhower  
> *(Slide p. 5)*

### 1.1 Four Core Activities

Essence frames strategy through four continuous activities:

1.  **Perceive** – understanding the problem and its context
2.  **Form** – designing the constructive parts of a solution
3.  **Consolidate** – integrating contributions into a coherent whole
4.  **Learn** – appraising and reflecting on the design

*(Slide p. 6)*

These activities are iterative and mutually reinforcing.

***

### 1.2 Levels of Abstraction

Essence distinguishes three abstraction levels:

*   **Rationale**  
    Logical basis for actions; enables reasoning about strategy and tactics.

*   **Strategy**  
    Overall plan addressing:
    *   Problem scope and context
    *   Essential parts of the solution
    *   Constraints and limitations
    *   Potential for future growth

*   **Tactics**  
    Concrete actions planned to enact the strategy.

*(Slide p. 7)*

## 3. Changing Conditions & the Need for Strategy

Essence assumes **continuous change**:

*   Inner and outer environments change as we learn
*   External factors affect project conditions
*   Master plans cannot be relied upon

Implication:

> Strategy must be **adaptive**, not deterministic.

*(Slide p. 9)*

## 4. VUCA Framework (Design‑Oriented)

VUCA describes challenges along two dimensions:

*   Knowledge about the situation
*   Ability to predict results of actions

*(Slide pp. 10–11)*

### 4.1 VUCA Categories (Table 10.1)

| Category        | Description                                                            |
| --------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Volatility**  | Conditions change rapidly or unpredictably; change is often understood |
| **Uncertainty** | Needs and solutions unsettled; change is known but outcomes unclear    |
| **Complexity**  | Many interacting parts; the whole is hard to infer                     |
| **Ambiguity**   | Causes and effects unclear; logic changes with actions                 |

*Adapted from Bennett & Lemoine (2014); reinterpreted for design challenges*  
*(Slides pp. 10–11)*

***

### 4.2 VUCA in Outer vs Inner Environment

| VUCA Type   | Outer Environment                                  | Inner Environment                                 |
| ----------- | -------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------- |
| Volatility  | Sudden changes in legislation, markets, technology | Sudden loss/change of tools, people, technologies |
| Uncertainty | Unsettled market, regulatory, or tech developments | Unsettled design choices or dependencies          |
| Complexity  | Many interacting external actors/systems           | Many interacting modules and components           |
| Ambiguity   | Unknown logic, needs, or constraints               | Design changes that alter system logic            |

*(Slides pp. 12–15)*

***

## 5. Complexity & Near‑Decomposability

Based on **Herbert A. Simon (1962)**:

A complex system:

*   Contains many interacting parts
*   Cannot be understood by analyzing parts in isolation
*   Often exhibits **near‑decomposable**, hierarchical structure

Key insight:

> Systems evolve faster when *stable intermediate forms* exist.

Reference:

*   Simon, H. A. (1962). *The Architecture of Complexity*. Proceedings of the American Philosophical Society, 106(6), 467–482.  
    *(Slide p. 16)*

***

## 6. The Outer Environment

### 6.1 Essence View

*   Environments are **not given**, but *made*
*   Often constructed by others:
    *   New technologies
    *   Standards
    *   Services
    *   Information infrastructures
*   Projects must begin with **assumptions** about an unknown environment

*(Slide p. 18)*

***

### 6.2 A Deweyan Perspective

Inspired by **John Dewey**:

*   Problems arise from **situations**, not isolated objects
*   Situations are meaningful contexts composed of:
    *   **Objects** (things with properties)
    *   **Events** (transitory, time‑bounded occurrences)

*(Slides pp. 19–23)*

#### Elements

*   **Existential**: real, physical elements
*   **Ideational**: conceptual interpretations (e.g., escape routes)

Both evolve through inquiry.  
*(Slide p. 22; Dewey, 1938)*

***

## 7. Four Outer Environment Categories (Table 9.1)

Essence proposes a **taxonomy** to structure exploration:

| Category                  | Description                                                         |
| ------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **External Services**     | Ready‑to‑use services that reduce workload (e.g., cloud, analytics) |
| **External Implements**   | Equipment, sensors, actuators via trivial interfaces                |
| **External Repositories** | Data sources for storage, aggregation, dissemination                |
| **External People**       | Stakeholders, professionals, authorities, user groups               |

Purpose:

*   Support divergent thinking
*   Identify opportunities and vulnerabilities
*   Reason about inclusion/exclusion trade‑offs

*(Slide p. 24)*

## 8. VUCA Strategies for the Outer Environment

### 8.1 General Strategy Matrix (Table 11.2)

| VUCA            | Strategic Response                                                 |
| --------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| **Volatility**  | Stockpile resources; force majeure clauses; *act–sense–respond*    |
| **Uncertainty** | Contingency planning; isolate dependencies                         |
| **Complexity**  | Reduce scope: fewer manifestations, users, dependencies            |
| **Ambiguity**   | *Probe–sense–respond*; safe‑to‑fail experiments; pattern detection |

*(Slides pp. 26–30)*

Primary reference:

*   Snowden & Boone (2007), *Harvard Business Review*

***

### 8.2 Probe–Sense–Respond

Used especially under **ambiguity**:

1.  **Probe** – Run safe‑to‑fail experiments
2.  **Sense** – Detect emerging patterns
3.  **Respond** – Adjust direction based on insights

Why:

*   Causality only becomes clear in retrospect
*   Requirements may *emerge*, not be specified upfront

*(Slides pp. 26, 30)*

## 10. Key Takeaways

*   Strategy is adaptive, not predictive
*   The Outer Environment is constructed, unstable, and partially unknowable
*   VUCA is a **design framework**, not only a leadership one
*   Structuring uncertainty enables learning, not control
*   Strategy emerges through *interaction*, *experimentation*, and *reflection*
