## 2. Horizon as a Guiding Concept (pp. 5, 9)

> *“We choose to go to the Moon \[…] because that goal will serve to organize and measure the best of our energies and skills.”*  
> — John F. Kennedy (1917–1963) (p. 5)

*   Horizon provides **direction beyond the current project**
*   Projects are not only **problem-solvers**, but also **capability builders for the future**
*   Essence encourages a **forward-looking mindset** even in small-scale projects

## 3. Four Core Activities in Essence (p. 6)

1.  **Perceive** – Understand the problem and its context
2.  **Form** – Design constructive parts of the solution
3.  **Consolidate** – Integrate contributions into a coherent whole
4.  **Learn** – Appraise the design and its outcomes

These activities operate continuously and iteratively across mission and horizon work.

***

## 4. Levels of Abstraction (p. 7)

*   **Rationale**
    *   Why actions are meaningful
    *   Logical foundations of strategy and tactics
*   **Strategy**
    *   Master plan for solving the overall problem
    *   Scope, components, limitations, constraints
*   **Tactics**
    *   Concrete actions taken to execute the strategy

Horizon thinking primarily belongs to the **rationale** level but influences strategy and tactics.

***

## 5. Pathways and Deweyan Pragmatism (pp. 10–12)

Drawing on **John Dewey’s theory of inquiry**:

*   Inquiry is guided by an **end-in-view**
*   Without a clear end-in-view:
    *   Observation lacks purpose
    *   Evidence cannot be evaluated meaningfully
*   All inquiry involves **judgments of practice**
*   Evidence must demonstrate **observable change** toward resolving a determinate situation

**Key implication:**  
Horizon hypotheses clarify **what to look for** and **how to judge progress**.

***

## 6. End-in-View in Essence (p. 13)

Essence distinguishes between two types:

1.  **Mission End-in-View**
    *   Pertains to the current project
    *   Tactical focus
2.  **Horizon End-in-View**
    *   Prepares for a future beyond the project
    *   Strategic focus

Both are:

*   Often distant
*   Uncertain
*   Subject to revision

***

## 7. Horizons in Essence (pp. 14–15)

Essence defines **three horizons**:

| Horizon | Description                                                                     |
| ------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **H1**  | Business as usual — current practices                                           |
| **H2**  | Transitional phase — clashes of ideas and emerging alternatives                 |
| **H3**  | Long-term future — new technologies, norms, values, applications, and economics |

Horizon analysis asks how today’s design decisions position the project across these horizons.

## 10. Technology Push (pp. 18–20)

**Definition:**

*   Change driven by advances in **technology and science**

Key questions:

*   Should future leverage be based on emerging technologies?
*   Will new technologies:
    *   Create new leverage points?
    *   Threaten existing ones?
    *   Enable superior problem handling?

**Examples discussed:**

*   Artificial Intelligence (e.g., code generation, refactoring)
*   Quantum computing
*   VR / AR
*   Microservices architectures
*   Accessibility technologies
*   Brain–Computer Interfaces (BCI)

Technology push affects **what becomes possible**.

***

## 11. Market Pull (pp. 21–23)

**Definition:**

*   Change driven by **users, markets, economics, and industry forces**

Key drivers:

*   Business practices and logistics
*   Globalization and regulation
*   Growing middle class
*   Security and surveillance needs
*   Critical infrastructure dependencies

Core questions:

*   Do these changes create new opportunities?
*   Do they reduce the relevance of existing solutions?
*   Will new markets emerge or old ones decline?

Market pull affects **what becomes valuable**.

## 13. Horizon Hypotheses (pp. 26–28)

### Purpose of Hypotheses

*   Ensure design serves its intended **end-in-view**
*   Provide a basis for:
    *   Reasoning
    *   Review
    *   Validation
*   Must be **verifiable or falsifiable**
*   Require explicit **criteria**

***

## 14. Rationale Hypotheses (pp. 29–30)

At the **rationale level**, Essence formulates hypotheses about:

*   **Problem**
    *   Is the problem well understood?
*   **Leverage**
    *   Do chosen technologies/resources make the solution stand out?
*   **Prospect**
    *   Does the solution actually solve the problem?  
        *(Ries’ “leap of faith”)*
*   **Warrant**
    *   Is the problem worth solving? Will the solution spread?  
        *(Ries’ growth hypothesis)*
*   **Backing**
    *   Is the solution attractive and valuable?  
        *(Ries’ value hypothesis)*

***

## 15. Horizon Hypotheses Framework (Table 9.1, p. 31)

| Aspect   | Present (Mission)      | Future (Horizon)                      |
| -------- | ---------------------- | ------------------------------------- |
| Problem  | Relevant now           | Remains significant                   |
| Leverage | Vital today            | Useful for future challenges          |
| Prospect | Solves current problem | Relevant to future problem categories |
| Warrant  | Worth solving now      | Addresses future valued problems      |
| Backing  | Competitive today      | Enables future competitiveness        |

**Goal:**  
Ideally, hypotheses should cover **both present and future**.
