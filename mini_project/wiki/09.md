## 2. Core Design Activities (Recap)

Four recurring activities frame innovation work:

1.  **Perceive** – understand the problem and context
2.  **Form** – design solution components
3.  **Consolidate** – integrate contributions into a coherent whole
4.  **Learn** – evaluate and improve the design

## 5. Evolvability

(Page 10–12)

**Evolvability** refers to the ability of a solution to:

*   expand into new contexts,
*   adapt to changing conditions,
*   and incorporate new leverage points over time.

Two dimensions:

1.  **Diffusibility** – ability to spread
2.  **Adoptability** – ability to fit and survive in new contexts

***

## 6. Diffusion and Diffusibility

### Diffusion

(Page 11)

*   Innovation only creates significant impact if it spreads.
*   Diffusion involves:
    1.  **Moving into new environments**
    2.  **Becoming established there**

Diffusion underpins economic growth.

***

### Generic Environments

(Page 12)

*   **Generic Outer Environment**
    *   Current design context
    *   Anticipated or similar future contexts

*   **Generic Inner Environment**
    *   Current system design
    *   Optional leverage points enabling adaptation

***

### Spider Plant Metaphor

(Page 13)

*   Innovation spreads like spider plant runners (“spiderettes”)
*   Each runner probes a new environment
*   Only some survive and mature

***

### Diffusibility Defined

(Page 14)

**Diffusibility** = ability to enter new environments and explore new application domains

Key mechanisms:

*   **Variation** – creating probes
*   **Selection** – identifying promising leverage points/domains
*   **Retention** – keeping probes alive long enough to mature

***

### Diffusibility and Fulcrums

(Page 15)

Diffusion potential depends on what your project is “anchored” to:

*   **Manifestation Fulcrums**  
    Similar problems across contexts → reuse potential

*   **Capability Fulcrums**  
    Capabilities useful across many tasks

*   **Merit Fulcrums**  
    New ways of creating value users will pay for

*   **Mission Fulcrums**  
    New challenges or limits overcome  
    → become innovation only through wide use

***

## 7. Adoptability

(Page 16)

**Adoptability** = likelihood an innovation can be integrated into new domains.

Key points:

*   Modular designs increase adoptability
*   Partial adoption is often crucial
*   Retention strengthens alignment between leverage points and domains

Adoptability depends on:

*   governance
*   integration effort
*   incentives
*   workflows
*   data
*   security
*   usability

***

## 8. Team Archetypes for Innovation

(Page 17 – Table 13.1)

Axes:

*   **Diffusibility**
*   **Adoptability**

| Archetype             | Characteristics                                         |
| --------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Pathfinders**       | High diffusibility & adoptability; best for uncertainty |
| **High Rollers**      | Many experiments, low survival                          |
| **Stable Performers** | Deliver well when direction is known                    |
| **Recreators**        | Preserve status quo; high risk in disruption            |

Pathfinders aim for **domain generality** and local adaptation.

## 9. General Innovation Theory

(Page 18)

Core references:

*   Rose (2010) – *Software Innovation*
*   (Supplementary) Tidd, Bessant & Pavitt (2005)

***

### Basic Terms

(Page 19)

*   **Creativity** – mental state enabling invention/innovation
*   **Invention** – new algorithm, data use, or software
*   **Innovation** – invention successfully carried into widespread use

***

### Innovation as a Combination

(Page 20)

    Innovation = Invention + Exploitation + Diffusion

*   **Invention** – creative act
*   **Exploitation** – practical/commercial adaptation
*   **Diffusion** – widespread adoption

Time lags are common.

***

## 10. Push and Pull

(Page 21)

*   **Technology Push**  
    New technologies drive change  
    (AI, IoT, digitalization, Industry 4.0)

*   **Demand Pull**  
    Societal or market needs drive innovation  
    (e.g. SDGs, climate change, pandemics)

***

### Incremental vs Radical Innovation

(Page 26)

*   **Incremental** → local change

## 12. Innovation, Economy, and Change

(Page 27–31)

*   Innovation drives economic growth
*   Rich societies are:
    *   more innovation-dependent
    *   earlier adopters
*   Radical innovation causes **creative destruction**
    (Schumpeter)

IT is often described as the **5th Kondratiev Wave**, continually renewed via AI, IoT, and data-driven technologies.
