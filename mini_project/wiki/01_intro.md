## 1. Purpose of the Lecture

*(Slides 1–3)*

This lecture introduces the **conceptual foundations of software and digital innovation**, the **Essence framework**, and the **roles and core activities** used throughout the course and mini‑project.

Key positioning:

*   This is **not a creativity course**.
*   Innovation is approached as **design under real-world constraints** rather than idea generation.

> “We only think when confronted with a problem.”  
> — John Dewey *(Slide 2)*

***

## 2. Digital Innovation

*(Slides 6–8)*

### 2.1 Definition

**Digital innovation** is defined as:

> *Products, services, processes, or business models that emerge within or between business units and where digital technology is a key factor in triggering or enabling value.*  
> *(Slide 7)*

***

### 2.2 Four Types of Digital Innovation (Table 1.1 – Essence)

*(Slide 8)*

| Type         | Description                                                                                  |
| ------------ | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Product**  | New or radically changed digital products or services                                        |
| **Process**  | New or radically improved ways of producing products or services                             |
| **Position** | Applying existing solutions in new contexts or markets                                       |
| **Paradigm** | Radical changes in mental models: what the business is, who users are, or what the market is |

**Focus of the course:**  
Innovations that create value for users or customers:

*   something new to known users,
*   something known to new users,
*   or something new to new users.

***

### 2.3 Characteristics of Digital Innovation

*(Slide 8)*

Key properties:

*   **Open** – spans organizational boundaries.
*   **Software‑intensive** – driven by digital technology.
*   **Transformative** – changes practices and enterprises.
*   **An extension of software innovation** – but with broader socio‑technical scope.

The openness of digital innovation is inspired by:

*   **User-driven innovation** (von Hippel, 1986)
*   **Open innovation** (Bogers et al., 2010)

## 3. Design as Innovation

*(Slides 9–12)*

### 3.1 Design Equals Innovation

*(Slide 9)*

> “Everyone designs who devises courses of action aimed at changing existing situations into preferred ones.”  
> — Herbert Simon, *The Sciences of the Artificial*

Design is therefore the **core innovation activity**.

***

### 3.2 Change as a Constant

*(Slide 10)*

Examples of persistent and overlapping change forces:

*   Climate change and ecological collapse
*   Pandemics
*   War and geopolitical instability
*   Cyberattacks and hybrid warfare
*   Generative AI (LLMs)
*   Information warfare and fake news

These forces contribute to instability and uncertainty in innovation contexts.

***

### 3.3 The Need for a Third Paradigm

*(Slide 11)*

| Paradigm              | Characteristics                     |
| --------------------- | ----------------------------------- |
| **Document‑oriented** | Top‑down, static, plan‑driven       |
| **Agile**             | Incremental, dynamic, user‑oriented |
| **Pragmatic**         | Problem‑ and solution‑oriented      |

The course adopts the **pragmatic paradigm**.

***

## 4. Complexity and VUCA

*(Slides 12–17)*

### 4.1 Factors of Change

*(Slide 12)*

*   Anthropocene
*   Knightian uncertainty
*   Hypercomplexity
*   Interdependence of problems and solutions
*   Innovation in a **VUCA** world

***

### 4.2 Hypercomplexity

*(Slide 13)*

**Hypercomplexity** inhibits rational decision‑making within reasonable time constraints.

Characteristics:

*   Interdependent systems
*   Open boundaries
*   Emerging digital ecologies
*   Rapidly expanding technological possibilities
*   Increasing system intelligence

***

### 4.3 Inner and Outer Environment

*(Slide 15)*

| Concept               | Meaning                                                  |
| --------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Inner Environment** | What we build — substance and organization of the design |
| **Outer Environment** | What we build for — context where the solution operates  |

Problems and solutions **co‑evolve**.

***

### 4.4 VUCA Dimensions

*(Slide 16)*

*   **Volatility:** Rapid or unpredictable change
*   **Uncertainty:** Unsettled needs and solutions
*   **Complexity:** Many interrelated parts
*   **Ambiguity:** Unclear cause–effect relationships

## 6. Essence Roles

*(Slides 22–34)*

### 6.1 Why Roles Matter

Roles are **structural concepts** that:

*   support shared understanding,
*   mitigate limited perspective problems,
*   help simulate stakeholder viewpoints.

Inspired by problems articulated by Nagel (1974):

> *“What is it like to be a user?”*  
> *(Slide 26)*

***

### 6.2 The Four Roles (Table 2.1 – Essence)

| Role           | Focus        | Key Responsibility                 |
| -------------- | ------------ | ---------------------------------- |
| **Child**      | Situation    | Idea generation and reframing      |
| **Responder**  | Contribution | Technological responses            |
| **Challenger** | Solution     | Value reasoning and prioritization |
| **Anchor**     | Valuation    | Evaluation and decision support    |

> Only the **Child role is fleeting**; all others are permanent. *(Slide 26)*

***

### 6.3 Child Role

*(Slides 27–28)*

Characteristics:

*   Can challenge any assumption
*   Above responsibility and critique
*   Main source of ideas
*   Can be assumed by insiders or outsiders

Typical contributions:

*   Ideation
*   Detailing (wireframes, simulations)
*   Evaluation suggestions
*   Situation or contribution maturation

***

### 6.4 Challenger Role

*(Slides 29–30)*

Characteristics:

*   Represents customer or surrogate
*   Source of domain knowledge
*   Accepts or rejects solutions
*   Provides reasoning behind solution choices

Contributions:

*   Identify problem manifestations
*   Prioritize features
*   Define value propositions

***

### 6.5 Responder Role

*(Slides 31–32)*

Characteristics:

*   Typically developers
*   Majority role in teams
*   Provide technical responses

Contributions:

*   Suggest technical alternatives
*   Evaluate feasibility
*   Develop capabilities
*   SWOT‑like assessments

***

### 6.6 Anchor Role

*(Slides 33–34)*

Characteristics:

*   Not team leader
*   Facilitates evaluation
*   Shields team from disturbances

Contributions:

*   Method and tool selection
*   Fair evaluation processes
*   External representation
*   Maintaining team functionality
