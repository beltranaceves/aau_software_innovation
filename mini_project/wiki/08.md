## 2. Core Design Activities (p. 6)

Essence structures design work around four core activities:

*   **Perceive**  
    Understanding the problem and its context

*   **Form**  
    Designing the constructive parts of a solution

*   **Consolidate**  
    Integrating design contributions into a coherent whole

*   **Learn**  
    Appraising and evaluating the design

These activities recur iteratively as both inner and outer environments evolve.

***

## 3. Levels of Abstraction (p. 7)

Design reasoning operates at three abstraction levels:

### 3.1 Rationale

*   Logical basis that makes actions meaningful
*   Supports reasoning about strategy and tactics

### 3.2 Strategy

*   Overall plan for addressing the problem
*   Scope and context
*   Essential parts of the solution
*   Constraints and feasibility
*   Evaluation of future growth potential

### 3.3 Tactics

*   Concrete actions aligned with strategy
*   Executed to achieve specific ends

## 4. Contribution Terminology: The 12 Building Blocks (p. 8)

Essence frames design through interrelated concepts:

| Concept                     | Description                                                   |
| --------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Problem**                 | An idea of a situation to deal with; found via manifestations |
| **Outer Environment**       | Domain where the problem exists                               |
| **Manifestations**          | Objects, actions, or events reflecting the problem            |
| **Capabilities**            | Designed features addressing manifestations                   |
| **Inner Environment**       | Architecture and primary components providing capabilities    |
| **Leverage Points**         | Sources of significant comparative advantage                  |
| **Solution Qualifications** | Why limitations are acceptable                                |
| **Value**                   | Value offered by capabilities                                 |
| **Mission**                 | Tactical objective – solve the problem                        |
| **Properties**              | Strategic objectives – strengths and opportunities            |
| **Horizon**                 | Alignment with future directions                              |

## 6. VUCA Framework (pp. 10–11)

VUCA characterizes challenging environments:

*   **Volatility** – Rapid or unpredictable change
*   **Uncertainty** – Unsettled needs and solutions
*   **Complexity** – Many interacting parts
*   **Ambiguity** – Mixed or unclear cause–effect relationships

Based on Bennett & Lemoine (2014), reinterpreted for **design challenges** in Essence.

### Table 10.1: VUCA Challenges (Essence Adaptation)

| Knowledge | Predictability | Category        | Description                                         |
| --------- | -------------- | --------------- | --------------------------------------------------- |
| High      | Low            | **Complexity**  | Many interacting elements; overwhelming information |
| Low       | High           | **Volatility**  | Instability but conceptually understandable         |
| Low       | Low            | **Ambiguity**   | Unclear causes and effects                          |
| High      | Low            | **Uncertainty** | Future unpredictable despite known basics           |

## 9. Near‑Decomposability as a Strategy (pp. 14–15)

### Simon’s Watchmaker Parable

(Hora vs. Tempus)

**Key lesson:**

*   Complex systems should be composed of **stable subassemblies**
*   Near-decomposable systems:
    *   Have strong internal cohesion
    *   Weak (but non-zero) coupling between modules
*   Enables:
    *   Faster recovery from interruptions
    *   Adaptation under change
    *   Evolvability

**Reference:** Simon, H.A. (1996)

## 10. VUCA and the Inner Environment (pp. 16–19)

### VUCA Strategies for the Inner Environment

(Table 12.1 in Essence)

| VUCA            | Design Focus      | Strategies                                            |
| --------------- | ----------------- | ----------------------------------------------------- |
| **Volatility**  | Rapid change      | Build generic capabilities; microservice architecture |
| **Uncertainty** | Unsettled needs   | Information hiding (Parnas); isolate uncertainties    |
| **Complexity**  | Many interactions | Modularization; divide & conquer                      |
| **Ambiguity**   | Unclear causality | Experimentation (MVP); natural selection              |

## 11. Detailed Strategies by VUCA Dimension

### 11.1 Volatility (pp. 20–21)

*   Build **generic**, reusable capabilities
*   Design “lines of tearing” to allow endurance
*   Use **microservices**:
    *   Loosely coupled
    *   Independently deployable

### 11.2 Uncertainty (pp. 22–23)

*   Apply **information hiding** (Parnas)
*   Localize change-prone decisions
*   Minimize ripple effects

### 11.3 Complexity (pp. 24–25)

*   Modularize into **near-decomposable systems**
*   Split problems into smaller sub-problems
*   Address parts independently

### 11.4 Ambiguity (pp. 26–27)

*   Use **experimentation** (MVPs, prototypes)
*   Treat design as **natural selection**
*   Preserve stable configurations as building blocks
