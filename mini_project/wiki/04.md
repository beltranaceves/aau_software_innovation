## 1. Position in the Essence Process Model

*Slides pp. 1–2, 6*

The Essence framework structures innovation work around **four core activities**:

1.  **Perceive** – understand the problem and its context
2.  **Form** – design the constructive elements of a solution
3.  **Consolidate** – integrate contributions into a coherent whole
4.  **Learn** – appraise outcomes and adjust assumptions

Leverage belongs primarily to **Form**, but is critically informed by **Perceive** and validated through **Learn**.

## 2. Levels of Abstraction

*Slide p. 7*

| Level                | Description                                                      |
| -------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Rationale (Why?)** | Logical basis for actions; justification of strategy and tactics |
| **Strategy (What?)** | Overall plan, scope, components, constraints                     |

Leverage decisions are **strategic**, but directly affect **tactical capabilities**.

## 3. Pragmatist Foundation (Dewey & Schön)

*Slides pp. 8–13*

### Deweyan Pragmatism

> “Every idea originates as a suggestion, but not every suggestion is an idea.”  
> — John Dewey, *Logic: The Theory of Inquiry* (1938), p.109

*   **Ideas** are *anticipated consequences* of acting with certain means under observed conditions.
*   Ideas function like **hypotheses**.

### Transaction

*Slide p. 11*

*   Designers are **in transaction with** the problem situation, not external observers.
*   Action produces **back‑talk** (unexpected feedback), creating learning opportunities  
    (Schön & Wiggins, 1992).

**Implication:**  
Leverage points are **tentative forecasts**, continuously validated or falsified through use.

## 6. What Is Leverage?

*Slide p. 17*

**Definition:**

> **Leverage** = technologies, components, information, or human resources that act as **keystones** in an excellent solution.

### Key Characteristics

*   Enables **substantial advantage**
*   Can improve:
    *   Features
    *   Cost
    *   Flexibility
    *   Robustness
*   Must **differentiate** the solution  
    → **Standard tools are *not* leverage**

Leverage ideas are **forecasts**, not facts.

## 9. PCRT Analysis (Leverage Evaluation)

*Slides pp. 21–22*

### PCRT Factors

| Factor    | Question                                                       |
| --------- | -------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Power** | How powerful is this option for *this team* and *future work*? |
| **Cost**  | Are gains worth the cost?                                      |
| **Risk**  | Uncertainty, variability, failure modes                        |
| **Time**  | Effect on development timeline                                 |

***

### 9.1 Qualitative PCRT

*Slide p. 21*

Used to evaluate **one idea**, descriptively.

**Example (Trilateration – Technology):**

*   Power: Medium
*   Cost: Low
*   Risk: Medium–High
*   Time: Low

Outcome: questionable attractiveness.

***

### 9.2 Quantitative PCRT

*Slide p. 22*

Used to compare **multiple ideas across categories**.

*   Power: +1 to +10
*   Cost / Risk / Time: −1 to −5

| Idea            | Sum |
| --------------- | --- |
| Trilateration   | −1  |
| Smart cameras   | +3  |
| Thermal cameras | +6  |
| Trained staff   | +9  |

## 10. Leverage Scenarios

*Slides pp. 22–27*

### Purpose

To explore **how leverage points can generate different capabilities**, not just whether they are good.

Leverage Scenarios:

*   Use **2×2 quadrant analysis**
*   Explore **alternative contributions**
*   Based on **W5H2 questions**:
    *what, why, who, where, when, how, how much*

***

### 10.1 Vertical Axis (Choose One)

*Slide p. 23*

| Question | Examples                                   |
| -------- | ------------------------------------------ |
| **Who**  | Specialists vs on‑the‑ground staff         |
| **Why**  | Preventive vs reactive, generic vs bespoke |

***

### 10.2 Horizontal Axis (Choose One)

*Slide pp. 25–26*

| Question     | Examples                       |
| ------------ | ------------------------------ |
| **What**     | Integrated vs decomposed       |
| **Where**    | People vs infrastructure       |
| **When**     | Planning vs crisis             |
| **How**      | Passive vs active              |
| **How much** | Investment vs operational cost |

***

### 10.3 Example: ClusterDetect Scenario Space

*Slide p. 27*

**Axes:**

*   Vertical: *Who* (Safety specialists ↔ On‑the‑ground staff)
*   Horizontal: *How* (Passive ↔ Active)

| Quadrant | Scenario                             |
| -------- | ------------------------------------ |
| I        | Passive intelligence for specialists |
| II       | Active coordination for specialists  |
| III      | Passive support for staff            |
| IV       | Active coordination for staff        |

Each quadrant implies **different capabilities and inner environments**.

## 13. Key Takeaways (Exam & Report Use)

*   Leverage ≠ tools everyone has
*   Leverage must **differentiate** and **empower capabilities**
*   Evaluation (PCRT) precedes scenario exploration
*   Scenarios are about **design space exploration**, not prediction
*   Leverage decisions strongly shape:
    *   Capability scope
    *   Architecture
    *   Evolvability
    *   Strategic horizon
