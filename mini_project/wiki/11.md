## 1. Purpose of Merit in Essence

**Merit** expresses **why a solution is acceptable and valuable**, even if it is imperfect.

> *“Better a diamond with a flaw than a pebble without.”* — Confucius  
> *(Slide 9)*

Merit captures:

*   What value the solution provides
*   What limitations exist
*   Why those limitations are acceptable

Merit integrates **functional** and **non-functional** considerations into the PSC.

## 4. Four Core Activities (Slide 6)

Essence structures work into four recurring activities:

*   **Perceive** – Understand the problem and its context
*   **Form** – Design the constructive parts of a solution
*   **Consolidate** – Integrate contributions into a coherent whole
*   **Learn** – Appraise and evaluate the design

These map directly onto PSC reasoning and ETVX later.

## 5. Three Levels of Abstraction (Slide 7)

*   **Rationale**  
    The logical basis for reasoning about strategy and tactics

*   **Strategy**  
    The overall plan addressing scope, context, constraints, and growth potential

*   **Tactics**  
    Concrete actions aligned with strategy to achieve specific goals

## 6. Consolidated Terminology: The 12 Building Blocks (Slide 8)

### Functional Structure

*   **Problem** – Situation to be handled
*   **Outer Environment** – Context where the problem occurs
*   **Manifestations** – Observable effects of the problem
*   **Capabilities** – Designed features addressing manifestations
*   **Inner Environment** – What provides the capabilities
*   **Leverage Points** – High-impact elements enabling capabilities

### Strategic & Evaluative Structure

*   **Solution** – Why the problem is resolved
*   **Evolvability** – Ability to adapt or add leverage
*   **Merit** – Value and acceptable limitations
*   **Mission** – Tactical objective
*   **Potential** – Strategic objective
*   **Horizon** – Future implications and trajectories

***

## 7. Functional vs. Non-functional Requirements (Slide 11)

### Functional Requirements

*   First-order concerns (explicit in PSC)
*   What stakeholders primarily pay for
*   Key question: *Is the solution functionally complete?*

### Non-functional Requirements

*   Second-order concerns (implicit in PSC)
*   Concern **how well** the solution operates
*   Address risk, robustness, and dependencies

***

## 8. Functional Merit (Slides 12–15)

Functional merit evaluates **functional completeness and fit**.

### Core Concepts

*   **Value** – Functional value of capabilities
*   **Qualification** – Degree of fit between solution and situation
*   **Reservation** – Limitations of the solution
*   **Rebuttal** – Why limitations are acceptable

> Functional merits are **explicit parts** of the PSC Merit cell.

### Functional Fit Questions

*   Do manifestations cover essential aspects of the problem?
*   Do capabilities cover required tools/features?
*   Does the value explain why this matters?

### Formal Relations

*   Every manifestation ↔ at least one capability
*   Every capability ↔ at least one manifestation
*   Every capability ↔ at least one value
*   Every value ↔ at least one capability

## 10. Robustness (Slides 17–22)

### Definition

Robustness is:

> *“Insensitivity to damage or the ability to withstand adverse and unforeseen events … without damage.”*  
> (SEBoK, 2024) *(Slide 18)*

Essence expands this to include:

*   Solution design
*   Development strategies
*   Long-term adaptability

### Robustness Strategies

#### Architectural (Slide 19)

*   Near-decomposability
*   Graceful degradation
*   Cognitive load management
*   Automation with user control
*   Error prevention & recovery

#### Operational (Slide 20)

*   Continuous monitoring
*   Predictive maintenance
*   Stress testing
*   Alternative process pathways

#### Adaptive (Slides 21–22)

*   Self-healing systems
*   Predictive analytics
*   Adaptive user interfaces
*   Multimodal interaction
*   Context awareness
*   Role-based interfaces

## 11. Dependencies (Slides 23–28)

### Definition

Dependencies are **vulnerabilities caused by external or internal factors outside immediate control**.

They may arise from:

*   External services
*   Platforms
*   Internal leverage points

### Risk Assessment (Slide 25)

*   **Likelihood**: technology, regulation, societal, market trends
*   **Impact**:
    *   Direct: core functionality failures
    *   Indirect: revenue, satisfaction, compliance

### Mitigation Strategies (Slide 26)

*   Graceful degradation
*   Circuit breakers & retries
*   Fallback mechanisms
*   Modular / near-decomposed architecture
*   Adjusting system boundaries

### Managing Dependencies

*   **External** (Slide 27):
    *   Abstraction layers
    *   Standardized integrations
    *   Boundary definitions

*   **Internal** (Slide 28):
    *   Dependency mapping
    *   Modularity & encapsulation
    *   Separation of concerns
    *   Information hiding

## 12. The ETVX Model (Slides 29–32)

### General ETVX Model (Radice & Phillips, 1988)

*   **Entry Criteria** – Preconditions
*   **Task** – What is done
*   **Validation** – Quality checks
*   **Exit Criteria** – Completion conditions  
    *(Slide 30)*

### Essence Adaptation (Slide 31)

Maps ETVX onto:

*   Perceive
*   Form
*   Consolidate
*   Learn

### Variants (Slide 32)

*   **White-box cells**: internal, transparent
*   **Black-box cells**: third-party services, outsourced components

Used to model robustness and dependency risks.

## 13. Abstraction Layers & Cascading Cells (Slides 33–34)

*   Abstraction layers:
    *   Hide implementation details
    *   Enable replacement of external components
    *   Reduce dependency risk

*   Cascading PSC cells:
    *   Changes propagate across problem, capability, value, and mission
