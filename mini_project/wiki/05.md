## Four Core Activities (Slides pp.5–6)

Innovation work is modeled using four iterative core activities:

1.  **Perceive** – understand the problem and its context
2.  **Form** – design the constructive parts of a solution
3.  **Consolidate** – integrate contributions into a coherent whole
4.  **Learn** – evaluate and appraise the design

> “Ideas are anticipated consequences (forecasts) of what will happen when certain operations are executed under and with respect to observed conditions.”  
> — John Dewey (Slide p.5)

***

## Levels of Abstraction (Slide p.7)

Three abstraction levels structure reasoning and action:

*   **Rationale (Why?)**  
    Logical basis for strategy and tactics.
*   **Strategy (What?)**  
    Scope, key components, constraints, and limitations.
*   **Tactics (How?)**  
    Concrete actions executed according to the strategy.

## Deweyan Pragmatism and Inquiry (Slides pp.8–11)

### Inquiry

**Inquiry** describes the learning process through which solutions emerge.

Definition (Slide p.10):

> *Inquiry is the controlled or directed transformation of an indeterminate situation into one that is so determinate … as to convert the elements of the original situation into a unified whole.*  
> — John Dewey, *Logic: The Theory of Inquiry* (1938)

Characteristics (Slide p.11):

*   Transactional
*   Open-ended
*   Inherently social

Inquiry combines:

*   **Conceptual operations** (ideas, hypotheses, ends-in-view)
*   **Existential operations** (experiments, observations, interventions)

## Ends, Ends-in-View, and Means (Slides pp.12–15)

### Two Kinds of Operations (Slide p.12)

*   **Ideational / conceptual**: understanding and envisioning resolution
*   **Existential**: actions that modify objects and events

### Ends and Ends-in-View (Slide p.13)

*   An *end-in-view* is an anticipated consequence of action.
*   Inquiry resolves problematic situations through existential changes.

### Ends vs. Means (Slide p.14)

Dewey’s distinction:

*   **End** = a sequence of acts viewed collectively
*   **Means** = the same acts viewed distributively

Ends and means are not separate; they are perspectives on action.

### Examples in Software Projects (Slide p.15 – Table 6.1)

Examples include:

*   **Material means**: components, platforms, infrastructure
*   **Procedural means**: sprints, planning, testing, retrospectives
*   **Ends / ends-in-view**: sprint goals, prospects, requirements, user stories

## Prospect, Warrant, and Backing (Slides pp.18–19)

### Prospect (Slide p.18)

*   A mental picture of a future solution
*   Overall **end-in-view**
*   May persist across multiple iterations or sprints

### Warrant and Backing (Slide p.19)

*   **Warrant** – why solving the problem matters
*   **Backing** – why the prospect is attractive and useful

***

## Argumentation: Toulmin Model (Slides pp.20–21)

### Toulmin Structure (Slide p.20)

Elements:

*   Claim
*   Datum (data)
*   Warrant
*   Backing
*   Rebuttal
*   Qualifier

Used to structure and justify arguments about solutions.

### Toulmin in Essence (Slide p.21)

Mapped to abstraction levels:

*   **Rationale**: Problem (datum), Prospect (claim), Warrant, Backing
*   **Strategy**: Outer Environment, Inner Environment, Evolvability
*   **Tactics**: Manifestations, Capabilities, Merit

## Solution Scenarios (Slides pp.23–29)

### Purpose (Slide p.24)

Solution Scenarios combine:

*   **Problem Scenarios** (understanding the situation)
*   **Leverage Scenarios** (understanding the contribution)

They compare alternative **prospects** using a 2×2 quadrant model.

### Axes Construction

*   Based on **W5H2 questions**
*   One question for the vertical axis
*   One question for the horizontal axis

#### Vertical Axis Candidates (Table 8.2, Slide p.24)

*   **Who?** (stakeholders, operators)
*   **Why?** (value, motivation, purpose)

#### Horizontal Axis Candidates (Table 8.3, Slides pp.25–26)

*   What
*   Where
*   When
*   How
*   How much

### ClusterDetect Examples (Slides pp.25–29)

Contrasts scenarios involving:

*   Safety specialists vs on-the-ground staff
*   Predictable vs accidental situations
*   Integrated vs decomposed solutions
*   Prevention vs response

Each quadrant represents a distinct Solution Scenario.
