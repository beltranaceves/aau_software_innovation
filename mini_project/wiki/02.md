## 1. Purpose of the Problem–Solution Canvas

**Essence assumption:**  
Essence does **not** assume causality, sequence, or linear processes. Problems and solutions may appear randomly, but meaningful design emerges when they are unified into a coherent perspective.

The **Problem–Solution Canvas (PSC)** is the tool used to create this unified view.  
*(Slide 20)*

***

## 2. Four Core Activities in Essence

*(Slides 7–8)*

| Activity        | Description                                   |
| --------------- | --------------------------------------------- |
| **Perceive**    | Understand the problem and its context        |
| **Form**        | Design the constructive parts of a solution   |
| **Consolidate** | Integrate contributions into a coherent whole |
| **Learn**       | Appraise and evaluate the design              |

These activities are **non‑linear** and often occur iteratively.

*(Slide 9)*

## 4. The 12 Building Blocks of the PSC

*(Slides 11–15, 21)*

### Overview

The PSC is organized around **12 interrelated building blocks**, distributed across **Perceive, Form, Consolidate, and Learn**, and across **Rationale, Strategy, and Tactics**.

    PERCEIVE – FORM – CONSOLIDATE – LEARN

***

### 4.1 Building Blocks (with Definitions)

#### **Perceive**

*(Slides 22, 12)*

*   **Problem**  
    A problem reflects an *understanding of a situation*; problems have no objective status.

*   **Outer Environment**  
    The surroundings where the problem exists and where the design will operate.

*   **Manifestations**  
    Objects, events, or actions that make the problem tangible.

> “If we mistake the problem, we will not inquire with relevance.”  
> — Dewey (1938), p.108 *(Slide 22)*

***

#### **Form**

*(Slide 23)*

*   **Leverage**  
    Keystones of the inner environment that provide an edge (cost, flexibility, robustness, etc.).

*   **Inner Environment**  
    The substance and organization of what is designed and implemented.

*   **Capabilities**  
    Abilities designed to handle manifestations and provide value.

***

#### **Consolidate – Rationale**

*(Slide 24)*

*   **Solution**  
    Why the problem might be resolved and why the result is attractive.

*   **Prospect**  
    The believed solution if used properly (end‑in‑view).

*   **Warrant**  
    Why solving the problem is essential.

*   **Backing**  
    Why this contribution is an attractive solution.

***

#### **Consolidate – Strategy**

*(Slide 25)*

*   **Evolvability**  
    Capacity to adapt to new environments or add leverage points.

*   **Diffusibility**  
    Ability of the innovation to spread or be reinvented.

*   **Adoptability**  
    Capacity of the innovation to be adopted by users or markets.

*   **Potential**  
    Strategic objective: strengths and future opportunities created.

***

#### **Consolidate – Tactics**

*(Slide 26)*

*   **Merit**  
    The *qualified value* of the contribution.

*   **Value**  
    Overall value of capabilities.

*   **Reservation**  
    Limitations of the solution.

*   **Rebuttal**  
    Why these limitations are acceptable.

*   **Mission**  
    Tactical objective: solve the problem.

***

#### **Learn**

*(Slides 31)*

*   **Horizon**  
    Future alignment and long‑term effects of the project.

## 5. Taxonomies Used in the PSC

### 5.1 Leverage Taxonomy

*(Slide 28)*

*   **Technologies** – scientific knowledge, machinery
*   **Components** – sensors, actuators, equipment
*   **Information** – data collection, aggregation, dissemination
*   **Human Resources** – users, authorities, organizations

> *Crafted interfaces:* non‑trivial integration into design

***

### 5.2 Outer Environment Taxonomy

*(Slide 29)*

*   **External Services** – used as‑is (e.g. maps, streaming)
*   **External Implements** – trivial interfaces to equipment
*   **External Repositories** – data sources
*   **External People** – stakeholders accessed directly

> *Trivial interfaces:* use‑as‑is

***

## 6. Hypotheses in Essence

*(Slides 30–31)*

Essence evaluates quality using **explicit hypotheses**, not assumptions.

### Examples:

*   **Horizon hypothesis:**  
    “The leverage is future‑proof.”
*   **Potential hypothesis:**  
    “The inner environment is flexible.”
*   **Mission hypothesis:**  
    “Capabilities are efficient and effective.”
*   **Merit hypothesis:**  
    “Cost/benefit ratio is attractive.”

> Hypotheses define *what success looks like*.

***

## 7. Fulcrums

*(Slides 32–33)*

> “Give me a lever long enough and a fulcrum on which to place it…” — Archimedes

### Fulcrum Types

*   **Manifestation Fulcrum**
*   **Capability Fulcrum**
*   **Merit Fulcrum**
*   **Mission Fulcrum**

The **chosen fulcrum** determines:

*   Where to start on the PSC
*   Strategy focus
*   Which blocks are primary vs. secondary

(Reference: Essence §§4.1–4.5)

## 8. Example PSC: *ClusterDetect*

*(Slides 17–19)*

### Problem

Safety can be compromised in crowded areas.

### Leverage

*   AI (DFNet)
*   Thermal cameras
*   On‑the‑ground staff

### Manifestations

*   Crowding
*   Congestion
*   Panic
*   Disruptive behavior

### Capabilities

*   Identify & handle crowds
*   Detect congestion
*   Locate panic events
*   Manage disruption

### Merit

*   **Value:** Low‑cost crowd detection
*   **Reservation:** Needs calibration & clear sight
*   **Rebuttal:** Acceptable trade‑offs for low cost

### Evolvability & Potential

*   Near‑decomposable inner environment
*   Generalized outer environment
*   Limited diffusibility but strong market reinforcement
